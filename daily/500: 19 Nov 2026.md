# Log 500: Joining Partway Through

> 19 November 2026

## What I worked on

- Reviewed the [recurrence stack][continued] and moved cancellation amount and
  quantity handling into InvoiceRequest support, with static-invoice rejection of
  recurring offers in offer support. Corrected compulsory-recurrence rejection on
  the single-payment path, the `c_bindings` builder conversion, and truncated
  encoding of the paywindow's `seconds_after` field.
- Changed offer expiry handling to prevent starting a recurrence while allowing
  follow-up requests, including cancellation, after expiry.
- Implemented proportional pricing for initial requests joining a period after
  it starts. Applied quantity before scaling, used widened arithmetic, and rounded
  up to whole millisatoshis. Follow-ups retain the full-period price.
- Separated preliminary request amount checks from the final minimum calculated
  at invoice creation. Construction and parsing share the calculation, and the
  automatic response validator returns the amount for the response path to use.
- Distributed the pricing work across five fixups and autosquashed them into the
  stack. Review then identified a duplicated timestamp-and-validation block in
  both response paths for removal. Compilation passed, including Rust 1.75 and
  `c_bindings`; behavioral tests were not run and focused coverage remains open.

## Reflection

The offer could already advertise proportional pricing, but the amount path
still charged for the whole period. My first reading treated this too broadly
as a discount for paying late. Working through the intended behavior narrowed
it to joining: an initial request can pay for the part of its selected period
that remains when the invoice is created. Later requests still owe the full
period's price. That distinction gave the calculation a much more specific job.

The difficult part was deciding when enough information exists to check the
amount. A request can be built before anyone knows its invoice's creation time.
At that point, currency, multiplication overflow, and supplied amount limits
can be checked, but the discounted minimum has to wait. Once the invoice time
is known, the calculation uses the selected period's actual boundaries and
keeps an explicit request amount only if it covers that minimum. Parsing uses
the same creation time, so receiving or reloading an invoice later cannot change
its price. Before the period starts, the price stays whole; once it ends, the
arithmetic returns zero. The payment window separately determines whether an
invoice can still be issued.

I spent several iterations finding a place for those checks that felt natural
to read. Folding preliminary validation into the ordinary offer validator
removed some repetition, but a `defer_minimum_check` flag brought optional
minimums and extra branches into a simple function. Keeping joining requests
on an explicit preliminary path was easier to follow. The final arrangement
leaves proportional arithmetic in `recurrence.rs`, amount resolution in
`invoice.rs`, and request eligibility in `invoice_request.rs`. Calling that
eligibility helper `joining_discount_terms` also says what the caller receives.

The automatic response path now gets its amount back from recurrence validation
instead of calculating it again immediately. Authentication still cannot settle
the time-dependent questions: a verified request may wait, its payment window
may close, and its joining price may change before invoice creation. Rechecking
at issuance remains necessary. Splitting the finished work back across the
stack exposed a more mundane problem after autosquashing: both response paths
had acquired a second timestamp-and-validation block. I identified those copies
for removal, keeping the first captured time and validated amount. Compilation
covered the integration, but the joining boundaries and amount checks still
need behavioral tests.
