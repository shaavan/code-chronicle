# Log 502: Across Invoices

> 21 November 2026

## What I worked on

- Reviewed the [dummy-hop privacy report][report] against the source without
  changing code. Confirmed that fresh dummy-hop fees and CLTV deltas can stand
  out across invoices when real forwarding policies remain stable.
- Narrowed the inference to the dummy-hop count and an estimate of the real
  route's aggregate CLTV contribution. It does not uniquely reveal the real hop
  count, and the report demonstrated neither fund theft nor payment failure.
- Compared stable padding per route, stable padding per offer, and standardized
  aggregate totals. Traced the remaining sampling or invoice-linking risks and
  the fee and timelock costs of making routes look alike.
- Drafted a discussion message with an upstream code reference and refined the
  comparison table. No mitigation was selected or implemented. The assessment
  remained source-based; I did not run the reproducer or any tests.

## Reflection

The report needed several invoices before the padding became recognizable.
Random dummy-hop policies sound useful when looking at one invoice, but real
forwarding policies tend to stay stable while the dummy values change each time.
Collecting invoices over the same route gives the payer something to compare.
The randomness itself can expose how much padding was added, leaving an estimate
of the real route's aggregate CLTV contribution.

I wanted to keep the consequence precise. That estimate does not uniquely tell
the payer how many real hops there are. The default offer's message path also
reveals some overlapping information, reducing what this adds in that setup.
Padding may matter more where it gives meaningful cover for the recipient's
location. The source supported a privacy weakness, though I had not independently
reproduced the reported behavior.

The possible fixes each made a different part awkward. Keeping padding stable
per route would stop fresh sampling across invoices, but matching totals could
help link invoices from different offers. Keeping it stable per offer would stop
sampling within that offer while leaving comparisons across offers for the same
recipient and route. Standardized totals could make different routes resemble
each other, at the cost of higher fees and longer timelocks, with routes above
the targets still needing a policy. I ended with a comparison ready for
discussion. Simply making the dummy values stable would leave another privacy
question to answer.
