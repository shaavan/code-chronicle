# Log 435: Local Enough

> 31 July 2026

## What I worked on

- [Continued] the currency branch by removing local FX-minimum enforcement for
  explicit `amount_msats` on currency-denominated offers.
- Reworked `Offer::check_amount_msats_for_quantity` so currency offers skip the
  local minimum while still enforcing `MAX_VALUE_MSAT`.
- Fixed the max-amount check to cover inferred bitcoin totals after quantity
  multiplication.
- Updated comments and `pay_for_offer` docs around omitted amounts, explicit
  amounts, and conversion support.
- Moved invoice-request amount handling toward `flow.verify_invoice_request`,
  where the payable amount is computed once and carried forward.

## Reflection

The currency branch stayed on payer-side amount handling, but the pressure was
different from yesterday. Yesterday's bug was about exactness after the
invoice comes back. Today was about the earlier question: how much should the
payer reject before the recipient has even had a chance to answer?

The reviewer point was fair. If the caller supplies `amount_msats` for a
currency-denominated offer, the payer's local conversion rate should not become
the minimum the request has to satisfy. The recipient may use a different rate,
and it is the recipient's place to reject an amount it will not accept. The
payer still has work to do, but that work is narrower: keep the millisatoshi
amount valid, require conversion support when the caller leaves the amount
out, and avoid creating requests that exceed Lightning's maximum payment
amount.

That made the implementation less eager. `pay_for_offer_intern` stopped
rejecting explicit currency amounts against the locally converted range. The
test changed with it, so a low explicit amount now proves that the invoice
request is still created. The more interesting part was inside
`Offer::check_amount_msats_for_quantity`, because removing one check made the
remaining checks easier to misplace.

I hit that when the max check moved too early. It handled some explicit paths,
but missed the inferred bitcoin case where the offer amount and quantity could
multiply past `MAX_VALUE_MSAT`. The better shape was to decide what amount the
invoice request is actually committing to, then check that final value. That
gave the comments something concrete to say too: currency offers have no local
msat minimum, missing quantity means no total can be computed, and
`amount_to_check` is the explicit or inferred amount that must fit.

There was a small history lesson in the docs. `pay_for_offer` still said
explicit currency amounts were checked against the payer's local conversion
range before sending. The code was changing that behavior now, but the stale
wording came from an earlier documentation commit. Moving the correction there
kept the stack from telling two stories at once. The docs now describe the
line more plainly: omitted currency amounts need conversion support; explicit
currency amounts are included in the request without local conversion-range
checking.

The other review thread pushed on ownership of the payable amount. Passing a
separate amount beside the invoice request into `flow.verify_invoice_request`
felt brittle because the two could disagree. Computing the payable amount
during verification and carrying that verified value forward gives the branch
one place where the request is interpreted. It also meant backing down an
earlier plumbing attempt: the `InvoiceBuilder` helper commit only keeps
conversion for the `respond_with` paths that directly compute invoice amounts,
while the invoice-request helpers wait for the later verification commit to
change their model.

This was a day of making the payer a little less presumptive. It still protects
itself against impossible or oversized payments, but it no longer pretends its
local FX view is the recipient's acceptance rule. That distinction is small in
code and important in the protocol shape.
