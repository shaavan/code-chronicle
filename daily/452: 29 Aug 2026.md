# Log 452: The Minimum Holds

> 29 August 2026

## What I worked on

- Traced the `compute_payinfo` HTLC minimum calculation through dummy-hop fee
  handling and `BlindedReceive` validation.
- Moved the payee minimum clamp after the dummy-hop loop while keeping the
  payee channel maximum before it.
- Added focused coverage showing that a `5_000` msat receive minimum stays
  `5_000` after three `1_000` msat dummy-hop fees, while the maximum is
  reduced.
- Tightened the comments explaining why the receive minimum must not pass
  through the fee-subtracting loop.
- Removed the redundant no-fee BOLT 12 payment claim wrapper and kept the
  fee-aware helper under the `claim_bolt12_payment` name.

## Reflection

The day began with a small-looking question in `compute_payinfo`: had the
payee minimum been clamped in the wrong place? The receive path made the
answer concrete. Dummy hops take their fees before the receive hop, and
`BlindedReceive` checks its minimum against what remains after those fees.
Passing the payee minimum through that calculation meant the advertised
minimum could be reduced before the receiver ever saw it.

The numbers made the bug hard to dismiss. A receiver requiring `5_000` msat
must advertise a final minimum of `5_000`. The sender or router adds the
dummy-hop fees on top when building the actual HTLC. With the old placement,
the derived-constraint case advertised `4_000`, leaving room for a sender to
construct a payment that would later fail at the receiver.

The fix kept the payee channel maximum before the dummy loop, where the
dummy-hop fees need to reduce the available upper bound, and moved the
receive minimum clamp after it. The focused test uses three `1_000` msat
dummy-hop fees and checks both sides of that boundary. The comments now carry
the same explanation: the loop subtracts fees from minimums passed through it,
while the receive-hop minimum is checked after that subtraction has already
happened.

The last part of the day was a smaller stack cleanup. The realistic dummy-hop
commit had already moved every test call site to the fee-aware
`claim_bolt12_payment` helper, so the older no-fee wrapper no longer described
an active use case. Removing it and renaming the fee-aware helper preserved
the explicit fee arguments while leaving the tests and behavior unchanged.

The useful result was a boundary that now matches the actual order of the
payment. Dummy-hop fees can narrow the maximum before construction, but they
cannot quietly lower the minimum the receive hop will enforce.
