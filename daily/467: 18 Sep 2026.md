# Log 467: The Stack Holds

> 18 September 2026

## What I worked on

- Reviewed the [recurrence PR][recurrence-review] as a six-commit progression from dependency adoption through durable state, serialization, storage, and manager tests.
- Corrected persistence and serialization issues around optional `initial_start`, BigSize boundaries, enum discriminants, and malformed records.
- Made recurrence updates reject stale writers through an atomic transition check, and replaced false restart coverage with a manager rebuilt from durable records.
- Repaired the payee-key recovery test, separated fixes across their owning commits, and restored the intended stack with interactive autosquash.
- Ran the feature-enabled and default library suites, recurrence tests, formatting, and diff validation.

## Reflection

The review kept pulling the work back from individual test failures to the shape of the six commits. A persisted `initial_start` value is part of the long-lived interface, so its optionality belonged at the point where the state format was introduced. A round-trip test could confirm that the current encoder and decoder agree while still allowing both to be wrong about an enum byte. The exact fixtures made that boundary visible.

The storage race was harder to dismiss as a test detail. Two callers could validate the same detached state and then overwrite one another, losing a cancellation, retry, or payment transition. Moving the transition check into the store mutation gave the durable record the authority it needed. The restart test had to earn its name too: rebuilding a manager from persisted records was the only way to test recovery instead of continued in-memory state.

There was similar pressure in the history. Fixes that crossed commit boundaries made the stack harder to understand, and the first autosquash left the fixups beside their targets. Splitting the changes and running interactive autosquash restored a sequence that matched the implementation. The final feature suite passed all 214 tests; the default suite passed 197 and hit one unrelated `Operation not permitted` failure in the VSS nonce test. Everything in the recurrence path passed, and the stack ended without leftover fixups.
