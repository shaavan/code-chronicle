# Weekly Log 43: Waiting on Confirmation

> 19 October 2026 – 22 October 2026

## Overview

The inbound recurrence plan became a store, then started taking responsibility
for accepted payments. I first reduced the record to what the foundation could
support. Claim handling brought reservations back, and cancellation and older
confirmations showed where the record needed to remember more than the latest
successful payment.

Most of the work was in deciding when progress could move and what had to
survive an interrupted write. Acceptance now reserves a counter before claiming
funds; confirmation advances it after payment success is durable. Cancellation
can stop new claims while leaving an accepted payment to finish. Compilation
passed, but the event-handling changes remain uncommitted and without tests.

## Highlights

### Giving the relationship its own store

- Implemented a separate inbound recurrence store with its own persistence
  namespace, startup loading, and node and event-handler wiring. Individual
  payments remain in `PaymentStore`.
- Made the relationship ID a stable 32-byte hash of the offer/payer pair,
  retaining both original identifiers in the record. Direct TLV serialization
  replaced compatibility scaffolding for the unreleased layout.
- Added lookup and listing through detached snapshots. The initial foundation
  removed pending claims and cancellation-only records, added `Completed`, and
  kept documentation within what the stored fields actually supported.

### Holding a counter until the payment finishes

- Added claim acceptance checks and persisted a reservation and payment record
  before calling `claim_funds`. Competing payments for the same counter are
  rejected while the accepted payment waits for confirmation.
- Kept the expected counter unchanged until finalization, allowing the claim
  representation to hold payment IDs without duplicating the counter. Removed
  stored `last_paid_counter`; paid progress is derived from the expected counter
  and last successful payment ID, including exhaustion.
- Ordered finalization so payment success is durable before one recurrence
  update advances progress, records the successful payment ID, and clears the
  reservation. Required persistence failures retain event replay, and user
  events wait until the required writes succeed. Recurrence-store locking stays
  ahead of payment-store locking.

### Remembering acceptance through cancellation and replay

- Added cancellation on the [inbound recurrence branch][recurrence-branch]
  under the same mutation lock as acceptance. Cancellation blocks new claims
  but preserves an accepted reservation; later success advances progress while
  retaining `Cancelled`.
- Restored required recurrence terms after trying optional terms for
  cancellation without a relationship. A missing record now requests replay;
  preventing future invoice issuance remains work for LDK request handling.
- Added durable payment-ID-to-counter acceptance history so older confirmations
  can finish payment-record and event writes without advancing twice or
  disturbing newer reservations. Existing successful payment records can help
  recover history from before that tracking existed.
- Narrowed rejection to safely failing an existing matching inbound payment
  record. Restored the shared failure helper and unrelated callers, preserving
  accepted, successful, outbound, and unrelated records without creating new
  records for rejected payments.
- Passed formatting, diff checks, and library compilation, including checks
  with and without UniFFI during the store and claim work. Tests were neither
  added nor run. Startup reconciliation, reservation release after confirmed
  failure, offer-term validation, and UniFFI API exposure remain deferred.
  The changed claim serialization has no migration from its earlier form;
  user notifications retain at-least-once delivery.

## Reflections

I started by taking state out of the plan. The first store did not yet handle
claims, and exposing pending claims or cancellation-only relationships would
have made its API promise more than it could do. Once acceptance was wired in,
the reservation had a concrete job again: hold the expected counter while a
payment could still succeed. Keeping that counter still until finalization
also let me remove duplicated state from the claim itself.

The order of writes mattered more than I initially expected. A successful
payment can be persisted while the recurrence still holds its reservation.
Replay can finish that update. Once newer payments have progressed, though,
the latest successful ID is no longer enough to recognize an old confirmation.
Acceptance history gave those older events something durable to check before
touching the current counter. The record grew again for a reason I could trace
through a specific interrupted sequence.

Cancellation made the limits of that recovery more visible. An accepted payment
still needs to finish after cancellation, and its success must leave the
relationship cancelled. A relationship that does not exist yet needs a
different answer in request handling. I also had to pull back a change to the
shared failure helper after tracing how far its effects reached. Keeping those
cases close to their callers made the remaining work easier to name.

I can now follow how acceptance, confirmation, and cancellation should interact,
but compilation has not exercised those sequences. Replayed events can finish
partial writes; abandoned work with no event still needs reconciliation.
Testing those cases and releasing reservations after confirmed failure come
next, before I can be comfortable leaving claims waiting in this store.
