# Log 486: Keeping the Attempt

> 28 October 2026

## What I worked on

- Made payer-side naming explicit across outbound recurrence storage while
  preserving TLV layouts. Added lookup and listing through cloned
  `OutboundRecurrenceDetails`, removing a separate snapshot type that added
  little.
- Added offer-aware amount and quantity validation, checked multiplication for
  the default total, and persistence of the relationship and initial attempt
  before submitting an initiation request.
- Routed initiation, manual next payments, and retries through a shared
  submission gate. It rechecks lifecycle status, attempt ownership, the pending
  payment record, and timing under the mutation lock before queueing. Retries
  get a fresh payment ID without advancing the paid counter.
- Combined outcome handling ahead of cancellation in the commit stack. Fixed
  inclusive completion limits, late success, repeated events, and partial-write
  recovery; failures arriving after success preserve the successful record.
- Added cancellation under the submission lock, retaining the abandoned
  attempt's payment ID for later outcomes. Local cancellation remains effective
  if queueing the remote request fails, with that error returned to the caller.
- Drafted the [outbound recurrence PR][outbound-recurrence-pr] around the manual
  Rust APIs. Formatting, diff checks, and library builds with and without
  UniFFI passed during implementation. Focused tests remain deferred.

## Reflection

I came back to the payer side with a store commit to review. The naming needed
to distinguish it from the inbound work, though LDK's `RecurrenceId` already
meant an outbound relationship and could stay as it was. Lookup and listing
raised a smaller question about how much public representation this needed.
A cloned record already keeps callers from changing stored state, so I removed
the extra snapshot type. Exposing that record through UniFFI would need a
converter for `Retry`. There were precedents for remote-type attributes, but
I left the new binding work for later and kept building through Rust.

The part that took more care was the gap between saving an attempt and sending
its request. Persisting first gave the node something to recover, but another
caller could still cancel the relationship or reserve a newer attempt before
submission. The gate had to check ownership again and keep the mutation lock
through LDK's synchronous queueing call. Moving manual payments and retries
onto that same path made the ordering hold across all three entry points.
It also exposed the timing check missing from an initial retry with an explicit
basetime. That check now happens immediately before queueing; a first payment
whose basetime is still unknown keeps its exception.

Cancellation made me stop and rearrange the stack. I was trying to finish it
before the outcome handling it depended on. Reading the success and failure
commits together made that dependency concrete: abandoning an attempt cannot
erase the information needed if it later succeeds. I put the combined outcome
commit first. Success now checks the period actually paid against the inclusive
limit, persists the payment before recurrence progress, and can finish a partial
write on replay. Older events must leave newer attempts alone, and a failure
after success cannot undo it.

With that in place, cancellation had a definite order relative to submission.
If it gets the lock first, the saved reservation never gets queued. If submission
gets there first, cancellation abandons the queued attempt but keeps its ID.
A late success can still record progress while the relationship stays
cancelled. Keeping that ID felt necessary once those cases were worked through.

The manual lifecycle is implemented, though the builds do not settle those
interleavings. Focused tests are still ahead. The audit also left a concrete
limit to address: the `u32` paid count cannot record another success at its
maximum. Restart reconciliation needs to retain reservations until outcomes
are established; scheduling missed windows and automatic payments, along with
shared UniFFI exposure for both directions, remain separate follow-ups. The PR
now has those limits written down alongside what callers can already do.
