# Log 478: On Its Own

> 12 October 2026

## What I worked on

- Reworked the [recurrence branch][recurrence-branch] into two steps: committed a public retry operation for one recurrence, removing the timer and bulk scan from that foundation. Retry reuses submission without advancing the period and returns a payment ID or an error.
- Implemented a separate scheduler that scans on startup and every thirty seconds, retries failed attempts, marks recurrences missed or completed, and pays future periods when enabled through a persisted setting. These changes remain uncommitted.
- Corrected the success handler's early completion boundary and shared completion and window checks across scheduling, manual payment, and event handling.
- Set automatic payments to target the period start, using window opening for early-only windows. Failed attempts can retry throughout their window; pending payments remain active after it closes until their outcome arrives.
- Persisted an attempt reservation before queueing, rechecked cancellation before submission, and let an ongoing submission finish recording its result during shutdown. A crash between reservation and submission still has no automatic recovery.
- Passed formatting, whitespace, and default and UniFFI library compilation checks. Restored unrelated formatter changes; tests stayed unrun under the repository instructions.

## Reflection

The retry loop from the previous work gave failed payments another attempt, but it left the rest of the recurrence waiting for a caller. Broadening that scan meant deciding when a period had been missed, when the recurrence was finished, and when the next payment should happen. I separated the manual retry operation first and committed it. That left the scheduler with a submission path to reuse, including the initial-payment exception for offers whose basetime is still unknown.

I expected window checks to tell the scheduler when to pay. Overlapping windows made that awkward: paying as soon as each window opened could send successive payments ahead of their periods. Targeting the period start gave automatic progression a steadier pace, with window opening reserved for early-only windows. A failed attempt still needs the whole valid window to retry. A pending payment needs to wait for its outcome even after that window closes. The completion boundary identified earlier was corrected here too, so the final allowed period remains payable.

The more uncomfortable case came from persistence ordering. If submission succeeded and saving the active state failed, the next scan could see something it was allowed to retry while a payment was already running. Persisting a reservation before queueing closes that route to a duplicate, though it leaves another case to handle: a crash before submission can strand the reservation. I kept that limit explicit in the proposed commit message. Compilation passed, but tests have not run and the scheduler is still uncommitted. There is more to check before leaving it to progress on its own.
