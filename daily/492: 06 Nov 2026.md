## Reflection

I came back to OMv2 with questions about what happens at the edges of a blinded
path. The route junction was especially awkward. Alice can choose Bob to reach
Carol without knowing that Carol's hidden instructions send the message
straight back to Bob. The path creator cannot see Alice's chosen prefix either.
A construction rule needs to account for that limited view. Checking known
identities gets partway there; the introduction node has to reject the bounce
when it processes the message.

The LDK questions took some reading before I could phrase them confidently.
Offline-peer interception already allows an application to hold a message and
forward it after reconnection. Dummy hops also had a concrete implementation:
authenticated layers before the receive layer, unwrapped recursively by the
recipient. The documentation explains their purpose in hiding the receiver's
position. Those references gave the review questions something firmer than my
expectation of how forwarding should work.

I also revisited the final-hop space I had questioned in the earlier reading.
The recipient prepares that data when publishing a blinded path; the sender
prepares it when building the whole route itself. That distinction mattered
when considering whether a sender could mark a hidden forwarding hop as final.
The initial claim that missing zero-byte validation was a security flaw was
too strong. Reading “interpret as final-hop data” as requiring the specified
zeros was reasonable, so I dropped the separate comment. The narrower question
remained: why reserve 34 zero bytes when the recipient might use them for
context?

Unknown path IDs needed similar care. The draft permits them, while preventing
a purported reply from relying on an earlier exchange without the expected ID.
The privacy explanation we discussed was an interpretation; the rationale does
not state it explicitly. Requiring zero for messages without earlier context,
checking known IDs, and dropping unknown nonzero IDs remained a design question.
By the end, I had fewer claims to make and more precise questions to leave.
Unsupported packet versions still needed an explicit rejection rule, especially
with the version byte outside HMAC coverage. I could point to that gap without
carrying the weaker findings along with it.
