# Weekly Log 45: A Place to Start

> 31 October 2026 – 02 November 2026

## Overview

The OMv2 plan gave me an implementation order, but I still needed to follow
the packet without skipping over its keys. I spent the first session working
through a concrete route, separating route blinding from onion construction
and checking what each hop can authenticate and learn. That reading gave me
somewhere small enough to begin coding: the fixed 114-byte routing slot.

Implementation has only started. Header processing, layered payload encryption,
HMAC coverage, size classes, and reference-vector tests remain ahead. The work
so far gave the first stage a concrete starting point without establishing a
complete packet flow.

## Highlights

### Following a packet through the route

- Worked through the eight-slot header and its filler. Each hop removes its
  first slot and appends filler, preserving the header size without exposing
  how many useful slots remain. Followed the zero next HMAC's role in
  recognizing final delivery.
- Traced a sender's route into a recipient-provided blinded tail, keeping node
  identity, blinded identity, the ephemeral onion `public_key`, and the
  route-blinding `path_key` separate. Blinded identities let the sender build
  the onion; encrypted instructions let the hidden hops find their successors.
- Returned to Diffie–Hellman to follow the two shared-secret derivations:
  blinding material and `rho_path` for forwarding instructions, then `rho`,
  `mu`, and `delta` for the routing header, authentication, and payload.
- Followed forward key derivation, backward packet construction, and hop
  processing. Distinguished the forwarding instruction's authentication tag
  from the packet HMAC, and the received packet's HMAC from the next hop's
  HMAC carried inside its slot.
- Checked transport overhead, feature advertisement, and route restrictions.
  Updated local BOLT references while preserving internal links, drafted a
  question about using the final slot's remaining padding for recipient
  context, and identified an existing channel-only feature needing a direct
  reference.

### Beginning with the routing slots

- Started implementing fixed 114-byte routing slots on the
  [onion-message-v2 branch][omv2-branch]. The rest of the first packet-mechanics
  stage remains unimplemented in this stretch.
- Kept existing Sphinx and route-blinding machinery in the implementation
  plan where applicable. Reference vectors remain the intended check once
  construction, processing, encryption, and authentication come together.

## Reflections

I could recognize much of the proposal before I could explain what happened
at a hop. Following one sender through an introduction node into a blinded
tail made that gap harder to skip. Every key needed a job in the example,
and every authentication field needed a packet or instruction it belonged
to. Going back to Diffie–Hellman took time, but it let me follow the
derivations instead of treating them as familiar names.

That also made the remaining questions smaller. The final slot has 32 bytes
for `path_id` and another 34 zeroed bytes; I could now ask specifically whether
recipient context could use that space. The unfamiliar channel-only feature
needed a reference to its existing definition. These were things I could
point to after tracing the format, alongside the broader questions from the
earlier planning pass.

Beginning the slot implementation felt manageable after that reading. There
is still a lot around those 114 bytes, and writing them does not tell me
whether a header survives every hop or agrees with the reference vectors.
For now, the next work is close enough to follow: finish the slots, then
bring the surrounding packet mechanics together and check them against the
vectors.
