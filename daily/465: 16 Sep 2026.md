# Log 465: The Storage Layer

> 16 September 2026

## What I worked on

- Adapted LDK-Node to recurrence-capable LDK APIs, including serializer changes, optional signature recovery, cancellation events, and documented unsupported payment methods.
- Added focused coverage for FFI recovery, legacy serialized data, recurrence-cancellation replay, and unsupported Bark and Cashu paths.
- Defined durable recurrence state for identity, offers, schedules, retries, attempts, cancellation, payment correlation, and transition IDs, then tested its serialization boundaries.
- Threaded recurrence storage and its manager through node construction, payment, event, cleanup, and wallet paths.
- Verified restart recovery, cache fallback, stale-index removal, invalid updates, CRUD behavior, and single-attempt concurrency.
- Reworked commit messages to follow the dependency-to-storage progression and separated two unrelated review findings from the intended changes.

## Reflection

The [recurrence review][recurrence-review] became easier once dependency changes stopped looking like the feature itself. They prepared LDK-Node for recurrence without enabling payer-side recurring payments. That boundary made the tests more useful: unsupported payment methods, FFI recovery, old serialized data, and cancellation replay each described a compatibility promise rather than a new behavior.

The durable state model carried more weight than its fields suggested. Identity, retry state, payment attempts, cancellation, payment correlation, and transition IDs are all pieces of what must survive a restart. Once that was explicit, the storage manager had a clearer job. Its in-memory payment index could be treated as an optimization because startup rebuilds it and cache misses fall back to durable storage. The single-attempt rule still had to live at the durable boundary, where manual and future automatic paths can meet.

I liked the progression from model to storage wiring to tests. It gave the commit history a shape that matched the design. The final review also caught two tempting distractions: formatting had moved a `debug_assert!`, and a candidate-lookup test comment belonged to funding selection rather than serializer migration. Keeping those out of the recurrence story left the dependency and storage commits saying what they actually do.
