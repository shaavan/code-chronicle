## Reflection

After working through outbound payments, I started by reading the stack as it
stood. Enough was already there that another list of recurrence tasks would have
hidden the remaining work. Marking implemented steps helped, but the inbound
path needed more than a matching store. Reusing a payer key across offers must
still leave separate relationships, and a cancellation can arrive before there
is any paid progress to attach it to. Those cases gave the inbound record
something concrete to represent.

The harder part sat between a payment becoming claimable and its receipt being
confirmed. I first had to follow what the events actually carry: the full
invoice is unavailable, and the offer context is missing terms and basetime that
Node needs later. That put an LDK change ahead of the Node work. Then the two
events needed different jobs. Acceptance checks cancellation and counters;
confirmed receipt advances progress. A payment can finish after its request
window closes,
so repeating that window check before claiming would reject valid work already
underway.

I spent time questioning whether pending claim reservations could be removed.
`claim_funds` gives no result to roll back against, and another payment could
arrive before confirmation, so the plan kept them. Clearing a stranded
reservation still needs evidence that the original payment cannot succeed later.
That remains open. The existing store mutation mechanism can handle the atomic
updates without another lock, provided cancellation and late events keep their
associations. The same care applies to `Missed`: late success can move progress
forward and recalculate the next window, while cancellation must survive. I left
the plan with those limits visible and an implementation order I could follow,
starting with the stores and leaving shared recovery and scheduling until both
directions are in place.
