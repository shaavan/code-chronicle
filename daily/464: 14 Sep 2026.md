# Log 464: A Map Before Motion

> 14 September 2026

## What I worked on

- Reduced the 31-commit recurrence PR to its storage, lifecycle, recovery, and application-facing responsibilities.
- Mapped existing LDK-Node structures against recurrence state, attempt tracking, scheduling, cancellation, and event handling.
- Defined review cases for failures, crashes around persistence, duplicate events, concurrent callers, and restarts.
- Reviewed the prerequisite chunk covering recurrence-capable Rust-Lightning APIs, serialization, compatibility changes, and cancellation events.
- Grouped the stack into four possible sub-PRs and recorded the remaining serialization and event-translation coverage gaps.

## Reflection

Reviewing [the 31-commit recurrence PR][recurrence-review] was difficult because it was too large to hold as one undifferentiated change. I started by reducing it to a sentence: LDK-Node adds durable storage and lifecycle management for recurring BOLT 12 payments. From there, separating CRUD from lifecycle logic gave the review a workable boundary. Records can be created, read, updated, listed, and removed, while payment success, failure, retry, scheduling, recovery, and cancellation determine whether those operations are valid and which state follows.

Mapping the new pieces onto `DataStore`, `KVStore`, `PaymentStore`, `EventHandler`, `EventQueue`, `Builder`, the node runtime, and `Bolt12Payment` made the ownership line more concrete. Rust-Lightning supplies recurrence protocol operations. LDK-Node has to persist state, coordinate callers, recover after restart, and expose something applications can control. Prepared and submitted attempt states, durable attempt claiming, basetime, and opaque payee state all follow from that boundary.

The review method came from the places where recurrence can go wrong. Every transition needs a normal path, failure path, crash-before-persistence case, crash-after-persistence case, duplicate-event case, concurrent-caller case, and restart case. Static inspection raised questions about ignored recovery results, unused retry timestamps, initial-payment retries, fulfilled-payment recovery, cancellation errors, and native versus UniFFI behavior. Those became questions for later chunks rather than conclusions drawn too early.

The first chunk was mostly prerequisite plumbing. The payee-key return type followed dependency compatibility, and the Bark and Cashu arms were acceptable as documented unsupported cases. The remaining gaps were narrower: serialization compatibility needed broader coverage across persisted types, and tests did not yet exercise the full LDK-event-to-event-handler translation for incoming cancellation events. Grouping the stack into four possible sub-PRs gave those questions a place to be answered. No code changed and no tests ran, but the later review had a sequence instead of a pile of commits.
