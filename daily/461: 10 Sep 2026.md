# Log 461: State Has Edges

> 10 September 2026

## What I worked on

- Added recurrence automation with recovery-gated startup, authoritative-store scanning, opt-in records, and protection against duplicate attempts.
- Added mutable routing overrides, including explicit clearing back to node-wide configuration, without affecting manual payment.
- Added configurable recurrence retry policy with persisted attempts, exponential delays, caps, and closing-window precedence.
- Added cancellation through `CancellationPending`, abandoning active attempts, preserving remote continuity data, and making cancellation terminal locally.
- Exposed recurrence inspection, listing, payment-window, manual-payment, cancellation, removal, configuration, routing, and lifecycle-event APIs in Rust and UniFFI.
- Documented the API and verified stable identifier conversion, replay behavior, default and `uniffi-default` builds, focused tests, and formatting constraints.

## Reflection

The work moved from recurrence state to the machinery that acts on it. Automation had to wait until recovery and event processing finished, then scan durable records rather than trust the rebuildable payment index. It stays disabled by default, can be turned off without removing manual payment, and must avoid claiming an attempt already being handled elsewhere. Fixed polling remains an awkward seam; exact due-time scheduling and fully atomic manual-versus-automatic claiming are still follow-up work.

Retry policy made that boundary more visible. Attempts, retry counts, and next retry times now survive restart, with three retries by default, five-second exponential delays, and a five-minute cap. The closing-window check comes first, so an otherwise eligible retry cannot be scheduled past the exclusive boundary. That ordering is small, but it keeps schedule state from promising work the window can no longer accept.

Cancellation required a different kind of finality. Active attempts are abandoned, the recurrence becomes locally `Cancelled`, and LDK receives stable continuity data only after at least one payment has established a remote relationship. Without a successful payment there is nothing remote to cancel. Late success cannot reactivate the record. Terminal removal then deletes recurrence metadata while leaving ordinary payment history alone.

The public surface grew only after these rules had somewhere durable to live. Inspection and control methods expose the state without making callers reconstruct it, while lifecycle events carry the stable recurrence ID, status, and monotonic transition ID. UniFFI needed validated hexadecimal identifiers and wrappers around internal LDK types rather than raw opaque values. By the end, the Rust and binding APIs were documented and tested, with the remaining gaps recorded as hardening work instead of hidden behind a finished-looking interface.
