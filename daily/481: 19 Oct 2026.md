# Log 481: What the Store Can Hold

> 19 October 2026

## What I worked on

- Implemented a separate store on the [inbound recurrence branch][inbound-branch],
  with its own persistence namespace, startup loading, and wiring through the
  node to the event handler. Individual payments remain in `PaymentStore`.
- Simplified the stored state around `next_expected_counter`, removed pending
  claims and cancellation-only records, and added `Completed` to the lifecycle
  statuses. The foundation now represents less than the earlier plan.
- Made `InboundRecurrenceId` a stable 32-byte hash of the offer/payer pair,
  retaining the original identifiers in the record. Used direct TLV
  serialization and removed compatibility scaffolding for the unreleased
  layout.
- Added lookup by offer/payer pair and listing of stored relationships through
  detached `InboundRecurrence` snapshots. Revised struct and field documentation
  to describe what the store currently supports.
- Passed formatting, diff checks, and library compilation with and without
  UniFFI enabled; existing warnings remain. Tests were neither added nor run.
  Event-driven transitions, offer-term validation, and UniFFI exposure remain
  deferred.

## Reflection

The inbound plan gave me somewhere to start, though the record became smaller
as I worked through it. Pending claims and cancellation-only relationships had
been part of that plan. Removing them made the current store easier to follow,
but left less for later event handling to rely on. When lookup and listing came
next, I kept their snapshots within the fields actually stored. There was no
reason to restore unfinished state just to expose it through an API.

The ID took a less awkward shape too. I had kept the offer and payer together
inside it, then hashed them only when producing a storage key because their
combined encoding exceeded KVStore's limit. Decoding that key always returned
`None`. Making the hash itself the ID gave encoding and decoding something
consistent to work with, while the record could still retain both original
identifiers. I initially carried compatibility code for the earlier nested
layout. With the feature still unreleased, direct TLV serialization was enough.
I liked being able to remove that extra work before it became something to
maintain.

Reading the comments closely caught promises the code could not yet keep.
Putting a counter beside a payment ID does not guarantee that they stay in
step, and storing timing does not establish what cancellation will preserve.
The outbound documentation gave me a useful model for describing the fields
without getting ahead of the implementation. Compilation passed, including
with UniFFI enabled, but that does not validate the future transitions or expose
these APIs through bindings. Persistence and public snapshots are in place;
the event handlers still need to give those records their behavior.
