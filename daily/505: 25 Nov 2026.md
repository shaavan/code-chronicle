# Log 505: At the Agreed Price

> 25 November 2026

## What I worked on

  `pay_for_bolt12_invoice` on the [currency branch][continued]. Both the method

- Added a shared invoice-builder check rejecting amounts that differ from an
  explicit invoice-request amount. Covered matching, lower, and higher amounts

- Documented the new `amount_msats` argument on both `OffersMessageFlow` invoice

- Folded five review fixups into their owning commits, then rebased across 796

## Reflection

The payment API's replacement looked close enough to its predecessor that an
important check could disappear during migration. The old method checked invoice
amounts; the new one leaves that to the caller. Verifying metadata establishes
that an invoice was requested, but says nothing about whether its price is
acceptable. That distinction needed to be visible both where callers adopt the
new method and where they leave the old one. I gave amount verification its own
heading rather than leaving the warning buried in the surrounding documentation.

Following the amount through the response path found a problem before payment
was even involved. The invoice builder accepts an amount separately from the
request, so a caller could supply a different value and still sign the invoice.
It would only fail validation later. Checking the explicit request amount in
shared builder construction catches that mistake before either signing strategy
can produce it. Requests without an explicit amount still need the separately
supplied value, so the check had to leave those alone.

The conversion documentation needed a different kind of restraint. LDK receives
no timestamps with cached rates, which leaves applications responsible for
refreshing them and refusing rates that have become too old. I also removed the
broad references to incoming async payments after checking what those offers
actually contain. Automatically generated async offers are amountless; including
them made the conversion policy sound wider than it was. Working through each
review concern separately helped catch that overreach.

Once those fixes were folded into place, rebasing brought the branch across 796
upstream commits. Some changes conflicted visibly, including signer and logger
types, the `PayerFields` rename, and moved test helpers. Others merged without
complaint but left callers missing the new amount argument or converter field.
Compilation caught those. I folded the compatibility changes into the commits
that needed them and kept the stack at eleven commits. The workspace, test
targets, and fuzz code compiled again, though running the tests remained open.
