# Log 482: Before the Counter Moves

> 20 October 2026

## What I worked on

- Added inbound claim reservations on the [recurrence branch][recurrence-branch],
  checking relationship status and progress before the generic claim path.
  Acceptance persists the reservation and payment record before calling
  `claim_funds`, rejecting competing payments for the same counter.
- Reworked `InboundRecurrenceClaim` to hold payment IDs without a separate
  counter. Removed stored `last_paid_counter`; paid progress is derived from
  the expected counter and last successful payment ID, including exhaustion.
- Changed finalization to persist payment success first, then advance the
  recurrence counter, record the successful payment ID, and clear the pending
  claim in one recurrence update. Kept recurrence-store locking ahead of
  payment-store locking.
- Preserved event replay after required persistence failures. Completed-payment
  replays leave progress and newer reservations alone; user events are queued
  only after the required writes succeed.
- Passed formatting, diff checks, and library compilation with and without
  UniFFI. Tests remain deferred. The correction is uncommitted; confirmed
  payment-failure transitions remain unfinished, and the changed claim
  serialization has no migration from its earlier representation.

## Reflection

The store from the previous day needed to start making decisions about claims.
Reserving the counter before calling `claim_funds` gave an accepted payment
something to hold while confirmation was still pending. It also made the
difference between a relationship existing and a payment succeeding harder to
ignore. The relationship is created when a payment becomes claimable. That
alone says nothing about whether funds have been received.

I spent most of the day questioning how much state that distinction needed.
The first version carried both a reservation counter and the next expected
counter, alongside paid progress that usually repeated what could already be
derived. Keeping the reservation counter seemed necessary while finalization
advanced progress before clearing the pending claim. Then I came back to the
order of those writes. The expected counter could stay where it was until the
claim was fully finalized. Once that held, the pending payment ID was enough
to identify who owned it.

Writing payment success first leaves a particular failure case to handle: the
payment can be durably successful while the recurrence still holds its pending
reservation. That is a state replay can finish. Advancing the counter, recording
the successful payment ID, and clearing the claim together keeps the recurrence
from getting partway through that step. An older completed payment must also
leave a newer reservation untouched. Working through those cases made the
smaller claim representation feel easier to trust, though compilation is still
the extent of validation so far. Failure handling has a state reserved for it;
the transitions still need to be written.
